# Patterns learned from the article corpus

Use these patterns for financial explanations and educational product copy. They are distilled from the four supplied T-J articles; imitate the editorial reasoning, not their wording or a single author's voice.

## Corpus

- <https://t-j.ru/btc-bear/>
- <https://t-j.ru/rwa-cbr-report/>
- <https://t-j.ru/indicators/>
- <https://t-j.ru/news/inversion-curve-assets-return/>

These pages are style evidence, not automatically current factual sources for a new text.

## Shared architecture

1. **Observable starting point.** Open with a concrete market event, reader problem, surprising relationship, or authoritative source.
2. **Practical promise.** Tell the reader what the material will help them understand, compare, or decide.
3. **Minimum foundation.** Define only the concepts needed for the next step.
4. **Mechanism in layers.** Move through cause, operation, effect, and interaction with other factors.
5. **Evidence with interpretation.** Place data beside the conclusion it supports; explain the denominator, period, comparison, or method when it changes meaning.
6. **Reader consequence.** Translate analysis into implications, suitability, risk, or a decision criterion.
7. **Boundary.** State uncertainty, data limitations, alternative scenarios, or who should not rely on the conclusion.
8. **Compressed finish.** Reassemble the main chain rather than merely repeating section headings.

Not every deliverable needs all eight stages. Preserve the causal spine that the reader needs.

## Explaining financial terms

Use a three-part introduction:

1. plain-language function;
2. concrete example or analogy;
3. professional term or formula when it helps further reading.

After a formula, decode every variable and work one small numerical example. Then explain what a higher, lower, positive, or negative result means. A formula without interpretation transfers work to the reader.

When comparing instruments, normalize the comparison: time horizon, currency, liquidity, risk, fees, taxation, and data window as applicable.

## Handling numbers

Give a number a job. It may establish scale, change, comparison, threshold, or uncertainty. Remove numbers that do none of these.

Useful patterns:

- current value plus an earlier value;
- absolute value plus percentage change;
- result plus a relevant benchmark;
- estimate plus source and uncertainty;
- historical relationship plus the limits of extrapolation.

Avoid precision beyond the decision. Keep material precision where legal terms, pricing, calculations, or thresholds depend on it.

## Causal discipline

Separate chronology from causality. `После` is not automatically `из-за`. When several forces interact, name the role of each instead of presenting one convenient cause as complete.

Use the chain:

`факт → механизм → следствие → значение для читателя`

If a link is inferred rather than established, say so. Offer competing explanations when they materially change the conclusion.

## Risk and uncertainty

Put limitations next to the relevant conclusion. Good boundaries include sample period, excluded costs, missing data, model assumptions, market access, liquidity, and the difference between historical behavior and a forecast.

Avoid ritual disclaimers that contradict the practical thrust of the text. The body and boundary must tell the same story.

## Tone

Write as a financially literate guide speaking to an intelligent non-specialist:

- calm rather than excited;
- concrete rather than ceremonial;
- confident only to the degree supported;
- explanatory rather than patronizing;
- interested in the reader's decision, not in displaying expertise.

Small conversational turns are acceptable when they reduce distance, but jokes, exclamation, or slang must not carry the explanation.

## What not to learn from the corpus

Do not copy article length, site furniture, first-person voice, investment calls, heading count, or a topical conclusion as a universal rule. Do not reuse sentences or distinctive metaphors. TheBrief learns the method: orientation, layered explanation, evidence, consequence, and boundary.
