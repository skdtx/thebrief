# End-client presentation copy

Use for a presentation whose reader is the prospective end customer. The text should support an informed purchase, not force a rigid slide template.

## Narrative jobs

Across the deck, make these answers easy to find:

- What client situation is this for?
- What is the product in plain language?
- How does it produce the stated result?
- What does the client gain compared with relevant alternatives?
- What conditions, costs, risks, and trade-offs matter?
- Who benefits and who should choose something else?
- What is the next step?

Order them according to the selling situation and the user's brief. Do not assume every job needs its own slide.

## Slide writing

Give each slide one decision-relevant message. Use a conclusion headline where evidence supports it. Keep body copy scannable, but retain the mechanism or condition that makes the claim honest.

Turn product properties into client consequences. Support a benefit with a term, scenario, calculation, comparison, or evidence. Explain necessary banking terms on the slide where they first matter.

Keep charts and tables decision-oriented: comparable bases, visible units and periods, and a stated takeaway. Move only truly secondary detail to notes or an appendix.

## Selling boundary

Selling copy may advocate, but it must preserve suitability, restrictions, risks, and the source of return. Do not turn a scenario into a guarantee or a best-case illustration into an expected outcome. Keep mandatory language unchanged and flag conflicts between clarity and approved wording for author/compliance review.
